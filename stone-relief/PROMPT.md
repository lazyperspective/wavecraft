## Two Faces in Stone

Make a single, self-contained HTML file: a portrait carved in low relief into a slab of stone, the kind of thing you might find set into the wall of an old museum courtyard, lit by a low sun coming from one side.

**The idea.** At first it's simply a face in stone, calm and still. Then, as the viewer scrolls, the stone begins to change its mind. The carving softens and reshapes, ridges flow and settle, and a different face emerges from the same slab. Scroll back, and the first face returns. It should feel less like one picture fading into another and more like the stone itself slowly remembering two different people.

**How it should feel.**
- **Carved, not rendered.** True low relief: forms that rise only a little from the background yet read as full, rounded faces. Crisp edges where the chisel cut, soft transitions where the surface was polished. The material should feel like real stone: grainy, weathered, faintly uneven, with weight and age.
- **Light does the drawing.** Grazing light from the side is what brings relief to life: deep, soft shadows in the eye sockets and under the brow, bright catches on cheekbones and lips. The light drifts slowly, like the sun moving across a wall through an afternoon, so the faces keep revealing themselves in new ways.
- **A transformation worth watching.** The change from one face to the other should be smooth, organic and continuous at every point of the scroll: no popping, no smearing, no long shapeless moment in the middle. Two clearly different people, distinct in character, who both belong to the same stone.
- **Quiet and atmospheric.** A restrained palette, a sense of air and space around the slab. Calm, contemplative, like a gallery after hours. Any interface should be minimal and graceful, just enough to invite the scroll.

**Constraints.**
- One HTML file. three.js loaded from a CDN.
- No external assets of any kind: no images, textures, models, fonts or depth maps. Build the faces from procedural geometry or a displacement shader; anything you need, generate in code.
- Soft shadows and slow light movement. It must run smoothly in a modern desktop browser.

Above all, make it something people scroll back just to watch change again. Use your own taste: the brief describes a feeling, not a blueprint.
