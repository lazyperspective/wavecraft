## Glass & Brass — a musical marble machine

Make a single, self-contained HTML file: a musical marble machine that feels like a beautiful object someone built by hand and set on a table in a quiet, softly lit room.

**The idea.** Marbles are released one after another, roll down a sculptural run of tracks, and strike tuned bars along the way. Each strike is a note, and together the notes play a short, recognizable, gentle melody. The music isn't laid over the top of the animation; the machine *is* the instrument. You should be able to see exactly which marble made which sound.

**How it should feel.**
- **Physical and believable.** Marbles have weight. They accelerate down slopes, carry momentum through curves, bounce a little on impact, and settle. Nothing should look scripted or on rails, even if under the hood it partly is.
- **Crafted materials.** Think glass marbles that catch the light, warm polished wood or brass, metal bars with a soft sheen. Good lighting, soft shadows, a sense of depth and atmosphere. Elegant rather than busy.
- **Musical.** Bars ring with a pleasing, bell-like or marimba-like tone and decay naturally. Tune them properly. When a bar is struck, the eye should see it too: a glow, a shimmer, a small vibration.
- **Cinematic.** The camera drifts in a slow orbit around the machine, as if you're walking around a sculpture in a gallery. Unhurried and calm.
- **An invitation.** It opens on a calm, still scene with a minimal prompt to click. A click starts the audio and the performance. After the melody finishes, it should loop or keep going gracefully, never just stop dead.

**Constraints.**
- One HTML file. three.js loaded from a CDN. All sound synthesized with the Web Audio API.
- No external assets of any kind: no image files, textures, models, fonts or audio samples. Anything you need, generate in code.
- It must run smoothly in a modern desktop browser.

Above all, make it something people want to watch twice. Use your own taste: the brief describes a feeling, not a blueprint.
